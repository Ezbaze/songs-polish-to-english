# My, Pierwsza Brygada: Translation Notes

## Stanza 1: The Legions

| Polish | English | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legiony to – żołnierska nuta, | The Legions – a soldier's song, | `Nuta` means a note or tune; “song” fits the collective military setting. |
| Legiony to – ofiarny stos, | The Legions – a sacrificial pyre, | `Ofiarny stos` is a sacrificial pyre, here a symbol of lives given for a cause. |
| Legiony to – rycerska buta, | The Legions – the brazen pride of knights, | `Rycerska` means knightly. `Buta` suggests haughty, excessive self-confidence; “brazen pride” retains both boldness and arrogance. |
| Legiony to – straceńców los! | The Legions – the fate of those who risk it all! | `Straceńcy` are people willing to risk their lives or face ruin for a cause. |

The Polish `to` (“are”) is omitted; the English en dash retains the compact form.

## Refrain: The First Brigade

| Polish | English | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| My, Pierwsza Brygada, | We, the First Brigade, | `My` (“we”) emphatically identifies the singers. |
| Strzelecka gromada, | A band of riflemen, | `Strzelecka` refers to riflemen; `gromada` means a group or band. |
| Na stos rzuciliśmy swój życia los, | Have cast our lives and fate upon the pyre, | Literally, they cast the fate of their lives onto the pyre. English rearranges the words and retains the image of sacrifice. |
| Na stos, na stos! | Upon the pyre, upon the pyre! | The repeated phrase emphasises the sacrifice. |

## Stanza 2: Suffering and resolve

| Polish | English | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| O, ile mąk, ile cierpienia, | Oh, how much anguish, how much suffering, | `Mąk` means torments or agonies. The repeated `ile` (“how much”) is preserved. |
| O, ile krwi, wylanych łez, | Oh, how much blood, tears shed! | `Wylanych łez` means “tears shed.” Polish leaves a fourth `ile` implied before “tears”; English does the same. |
| Pomimo to – nie ma zwątpienia, | Yet through it all, we did not waver; | Literally, “despite this, there is no doubt.” English expresses steadfastness through an action and changes the present tense to past. |
| Dodawał sił – wędrówki kres! | The journey's end gave us strength. | `Wędrówki kres` is “the journey's end”; English restores the usual subject–verb order. |

## Stanza 3: Disbelief, isolation, and the Commander

| Polish | English | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Krzyczeli, żeśmy stumanieni, | They shouted that we'd been fooled, | `Żeśmy` is an older construction; `stumanieni` means fooled, misled, or befuddled. |
| Nie wierząc nam, że chcieć – to móc, | Not believing us – where there's a will, there's a way. | `Chcieć – to móc` literally means “to want is to be able.” The English proverb conveys the same conviction; `nam` (“us”) is retained. |
| Leliśmy krew osamotnieni, | We shed our blood, standing alone, | `Leliśmy` is an older form of “we poured.” `Osamotnieni` means isolated or without support. |
| A z nami był nasz drogi Wódz! | Yet our beloved Commander stood beside us. | `Wódz` refers to Józef Piłsudski. “Stood beside us” expands “was with us” to convey solidarity despite isolation. |

## Stanza 4: Belated respect and retribution

| Polish | English | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inaczej się dziś zapatrują, | Today they see things differently, | `Zapatrują się` means to view or judge something; the critics' attitude has changed. |
| I trafić chcą do naszych dusz, | And want to reach our souls, | `Trafić do dusz` suggests reaching or affecting people inwardly. |
| I mówią, że nas już szanują, | And say they now respect us, | The repeated `i` (“and”) is retained. The respect is what the critics claim, not an established fact. |
| Lecz właśnie czas odwetu już! | But the time for retribution has come! | `Odwet` means retaliation or revenge; “retribution” retains the severity. |

## Stanza 5: Rejection of approval and appeals

| Polish | English | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nie chcemy już od was uznania, | We no longer want your recognition, | `Uznanie` means recognition or approval; `już` gives the sense of “no longer.” |
| Ni waszych mów, ni waszych łez! | Nor your speeches, nor your tears! | The repeated `ni` (“nor”) is retained. `Mów` means speeches. |
| Już skończył się czas kołatania, | The time for knocking at your hearts | `Kołatanie` literally means knocking, here persistent appeals. The English sentence continues into the next line. |
| Do waszych serc – do waszych kies! | And at your purses is over! | `Serca` (“hearts”) suggests sympathy; `kies` (“purses”) suggests financial support. English joins the two objects with “and.” |

## Stanza 6: Reckoning and retaliation

| Polish | English | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dziś nadszedł czas pokwitowania | Now the time has come to settle accounts | `Pokwitowanie` literally means a receipt or acknowledgement; here it suggests a reckoning for suffering. “Now” conveys `dziś` (“today”). |
| Za mękę serc i katusz dni, | For hearts in anguish and days of torment; | `Męka serc` is heartfelt agony; `katusze dni` evokes prolonged torment. |
| Nie chciejcie więc – politowania, | So do not expect pity; | `Nie chciejcie` means “do not seek/want”; “expect” makes the warning natural. `Więc` means “so,” and `politowanie` means pity. |
| Zasadą jest: za krew – chciej krwi! | The rule is: for blood – demand blood! | `Chciej` is an imperative, literally “want”; “demand” conveys the force. The colon and en dash retain the rhetorical division. |

## Stanza 7: Building Poland and the judgment of posterity

| Polish | English | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dzisiaj już my – jednością silni, | Today we – strong in unity – | `My` (“we”) is emphatic; `jednością silni` means strong through unity. The sentence continues into the next line. |
| Tworzymy Polskę – przodków mit, | Are building Poland – our ancestors' vision. | `Tworzymy` means “we create/build.” `Mit` literally means “myth,” here an inherited national ideal; “vision” avoids an unintended sense of falsehood. |
| Że wy w tej pracy nie dość pilni, | In this work, you are not diligent enough; | `Pilni` means diligent or conscientious, rather than simply hardworking. The connection introduced by `że` is conveyed by the sentence structure, without an explicit “because.” |
| Zostanie wam potomnych wstyd! | Future generations will remember you with shame! | Literally, the shame of posterity will remain with the addressees. “Remember” is implied; it conveys enduring disgrace in future generations' eyes. |

## Stanza 8: Youthful belief and sacrifice

| Polish | English | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Umieliśmy w ogień zapału | We knew how to fan the sparks | `Umieliśmy` means “we knew how.” The first two Polish lines use inverted word order, rearranged for English. |
| Młodzieńczych wiar rozniecić skry, | Of youthful convictions into a fire of zeal, | `Skry` is a poetic form of “sparks”; `wiar` (“beliefs/faiths”) is plural. “Convictions” preserves that number, and `ogień zapału` is a fire of zeal. |
| Nieść życie swe dla ideału | To offer our lives for an ideal, | `Nieść` literally means “to bring/carry.” “Offer” conveys voluntary sacrifice. |
| I swoją krew, i marzeń sny. | Our blood, and the dreams we cherished. | Polish repeats `i` (“and”); English omits the first for a smoother list. `Marzeń sny` combines dreams and aspirations; “dreams we cherished” conveys the idea without literal repetition. |

## Stanza 9: Sacrifice for future generations

| Polish | English | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Potrafim dziś dla potomności | Even today, for posterity, | `Potrafim` is an older form meaning “we can”; `potomność` means posterity. “Even today” contrasts continued willingness with the past ability described in stanza 8. |
| Ostatki swych poświęcić dni, | We can sacrifice the last of our days, | `Ostatki` means the last remnants; `poświęcić` means to devote or sacrifice. Both ideas are retained. |
| Wśród fałszów siać siew szlachetności, | And sow seeds of nobility amid falsehoods | `Fałszów` is plural (“falsehoods/deceit”). `Szlachetność` means moral nobility, not social rank. `Siać siew` repeats “sow/sowing”; “and” coordinates this act with sacrificing one's days. |
| Miazgą swych ciał, żarem swej krwi. | With our bodies crushed to pulp, with the fire of our blood. | `Miazga` is a crushed mass; `żar` suggests heat or fervour. Both instrumental phrases describe the cost and intensity of sacrifice. |
